# Rubric: short portfolio writeup of a machine-learning project

The author was asked for a short portfolio writeup (roughly 150-250 words, one or two short paragraphs, no heading) of "Pitcher Injury Risk+ (PIR)", a machine-learning system predicting MLB pitcher injury risk from workload and biomechanics data. The owner built the feature pipeline (rolling workload windows, an acute:chronic workload ratio) and the evaluation setup, and used a time-based split to avoid leakage. It should read in the owner's own voice for their portfolio site.

Judge only the artifact.

## Scale
- 5: Specific and evidence-driven: states what was built and why it is hard, using the details supplied (rolling windows, acute:chronic ratio, time-based split against leakage) and explaining the reasoning behind a choice. First person, plain, restrained voice with no marketing language. Adds no invented facts (no made-up metrics, file names, datasets or results). One or two short paragraphs within the requested length.
- 4: Same, with minor weaknesses: one vague sentence, slightly over or under length, or a small amount of stock phrasing.
- 3: Accurate and in the right voice but mostly a list of what was done with little reasoning, or it invents one or two plausible-sounding specifics.
- 2: Generic or promotional in tone ("cutting-edge", "passionate", "powerful"), or several invented specifics presented as fact.
- 1: Reads like marketing copy or a press release; supplied details barely used.
- 0: Empty, off-topic, not a writeup, or fabricates results throughout.

## Does not earn points
- Buzzwords and superlatives instead of evidence.
- Numbers, results, tools, file names or validation steps that the prompt did not provide and the author could not know.
- Headings, bullet lists or a title when none was requested.
- Third-person bio language ("he is a passionate engineer").
